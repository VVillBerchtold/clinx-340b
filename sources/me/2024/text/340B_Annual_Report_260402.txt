340B Drug Program Transparency Report
Title 22, §1728, requires the MHDO to produce and post on its publicly accessible website a report
that includes a summary of the aggregate information received from hospitals participating in the
federal drug pricing program under Section 340B of the federal Public Health Service Act, 42
United States Code, Section 256b, referred to in this section as “the 340B program”.
The reporting requirements are deﬁned in 90-590 Chapter 340, Uniform Reporting System for
Reporting 340B Drug Program Data Sets. This is MHDO’s ﬁrst annual report.
Under contract with MHDO, Ten2Eleven Business Solutions provided MHDO technical support in
the preparation of this report.

Introduction
The 340B Drug Program is codiﬁed in Section 340B of the federal Public Health Service Act and
requires pharmaceutical manufacturers participating in Medicaid to sell outpatient drugs at
discounted prices to health care organizations that care for many uninsured and low-income
patients. These organizations include federal grantee organizations and several types of hospitals,
including critical access hospitals (CAHs), sole community hospitals (SCHs), rural referral centers
(RRCs), and public and nonproﬁt disproportionate share hospitals (DSHs) that serve low-income
and indigent populations.
Title 22, §1728 requires hospitals operating in the State that participate in the 340B Drug Program
to provide summary information regarding the annual cost to acquire prescription drugs under the
340B Drug Program and the estimated amount of savings realized by the hospital that result from
the discounted 340B drug prices. In addition, hospitals must describe how they use savings
generated from participation in the 340B Drug Program to provide programs and services that
beneﬁt their community, including services that support community access to care that the
hospital could not continue without such savings. Finally, hospitals must describe internal review
and oversight processes used to meet the federal Department of Health and Human Services,
Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) program rules and guidance for
compliance.

340B Drug Acquisition Costs and Estimated Savings
A total of 27 hospitals submitted 340B Drug Program data sets to the MHDO for ﬁscal year 2024
per the requirements in 90-590 Chapter 340, Uniform Reporting System for Reporting 340B Drug
Program Data Sets. In total, these hospitals reported spending $409.9M to acquire prescription
drugs under the 340B Drug Program, representing 60.0% of the total amount spent for all drugs by
the hospitals (included those purchased under 340B). The hospitals also reported an estimated
savings of $325.8M resulting from 340B discounted drug acquisition costs.
As reported, 340B estimated savings represents the cost that would have otherwise been paid to
acquire drugs purchased under the 340B Drug Program had a 340B discount not been applied
(based on the average acquisition cost paid for the same drugs outside the 340B program on a per
unit basis), reduced by:

Bridgton Hospital
  Behavioral Health Services

    to access behavioral health medical and consultative services in the inpatient and

    care practices to help support the behavioral health needs of our patients and community.

    health care need is even more vital.
  Financial Assistance/Advocacy
     Our mission is to provide access to medically necessary health care to all patients,

340B Drug Program Transparency Reporting Changes for Fisal Year 2025
Title 22, §1728 was amended in 2025 to require hospitals to report additional data elements
beginning with ﬁscal year 2025 reports. The new data will provide insight into the amount of 340B
savings that are used to pay 340B contract pharmacies, third party administrators, and other

Conclusion
This ﬁrst annual 340B Drug Program Transparency Report provides baseline information regarding
hospitals in Maine that participate in the 340B Drug Program, the amount of savings recognized by
the hospitals that result from participation, and the types of programs and services each hospital
offers that are funded in part by 340B Drug Program savings.
The MHDO’s second report will be published following implementation of expanded reporting
requirements in 2026 and include additional detail the costs associated with administering the
program, payments received from payers for drugs dispensed or administered under the program,
and insights into the drugs that are most frequently dispensed and administered drugs by
hospitals under the 340B Drug Program in the state of Maine.
